# What problem statements and projects win corporate and fintech AI hackathons (India-focused, 2023-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-12. About 35 tool calls. Several primary pages returned 403 (Paytm Medium blog, YourStory, Mastercard, GitHub-adjacent), so some claims rest on search-engine summaries. These are flagged. No official, published winners list was found for any 2026 Paytm Build for India edition, so every claim about "who won at Paytm" is a gap, not a finding.

## Q0 (context). What is the Paytm event this research is feeding, and what is publicly known about it?

### Takeaway
Paytm's 2026 "Build for India AI Hackathon" is a multi-city series (Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Chennai) with a three-track structure: Merchant Growth AI, AI-Powered Financial Journeys, and Autonomous AI Teammates (sales/customer service). Finals require a working prototype plus demo video. Judging criteria and winners are not published anywhere I could reach.

### Cited Findings
- Bengaluru finals (31 Oct 2026, offline): Track 1 "Merchant Growth AI" ("Build the AI business partner for every Paytm merchant"); Track 2 "AI-Powered Financial Journeys" (insurance, lending, fintech friction); Track 3 "Autonomous AI Teammates" ("an AI that can make decisions, take actions, and deliver measurable outcomes in sales or customer service"). Stage 1 = pitch deck due 27 Oct (title, problem, solution, tech stack, USP, impact/benefits, business model); Stage 2 = offline build of a working prototype/PoC with mentors, submit prototype + demo video + presentation. Judging criteria "will be shared with shortlisted teams later"; no judges or winners listed. Organised by Paytm with devSemble. — [Luma, Paytm Build for India AI Hackathon - Bengaluru](https://luma.com/6afp5q46)
- An earlier Bengaluru build sprint (21 Mar 2026, invite-only, Paytm office, teams of 1-3, Rs 1,00,000 prize pool) had two tracks: "AI for Paytm Users" and "AI for Small Businesses". Criteria not stated. — [NAMESPACE Substack](https://namespaceworld.substack.com/p/hottest-updates-paytm-ai-fintech)
- Mumbai edition listed for 3 Oct 2026 at Paytm office with partners Mochatrade, n8n, Sarvam, Cognee — [Hackbriven](https://hackbriven.com/event/paytm-build-for-india-ai-hackathon-mumbai-edition). A HackCulture listing shows Rs 40,000 prize pool and 1,472 registrations but dates (22 May-6 Jun 2026) that conflict with Hackbriven — [HackCulture](https://hackculture.io/hackathons/paytm-ai-hackathon) (page body itself returned no details; figures come from search summary). Other editions (Delhi, Hyderabad 13-14 Nov 2026, Chennai) exist — [devSemble Hyderabad](https://devsemble.com/hackathons/paytm-build-for-india-ai-hackathon-hyderabad-by-paytm), [Hackbriven Delhi](https://hackbriven.com/event/paytm-build-for-india-ai-hackathon-delhi-edition).
- Finalist signals only (not winners): a LinkedIn post says team "Kohinoor" reached Top 10 in the Merchant Growth AI track at Mumbai and was among Top 30 in that track — [Shubham Jha, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/shubham-jha-b58964166/) (self-reported, via search summary). An earlier search summary cited a LinkedIn post claiming ~6,000 registrations, 100 teams in the offline finale, 10 teams on stage, one winner ("Saathi"); I could not open or verify that post, and it is self-reported.

Pitch line: "Other voice bots wait for questions. Saathi hunts for lost money and acts." — [github.com/amithrh/saathi-paytm-hackathon](https://github.com/amithrh/saathi-paytm-hackathon)

### Inferences
- The Track 3 wording ("make decisions, take actions, deliver measurable outcomes") and the Stage 1 template ("impact and benefits", "business model") tell you what Paytm's judges will probably score: action-taking, quantified outcome, monetisable for Paytm. This is inference from the brief, not published criteria.
- Both public repos pair merchant-side data (Soundbox, Payment Gateway settlements) with an action loop (retry settlement, draft restock order, offer loan) rather than an analytics dashboard. That is consistent with the "AI business partner for merchants" framing.

### Gaps
- No official winners, scores, judge names or criteria for any 2026 Paytm edition. Paytm's Medium "Judgement Day" post (older, NFC-ring hack "SEVEN" per search summary) returned 403 and predates the AI era.
- Registrations/finalist counts for Mumbai could not be verified.

## Q1. Which problem-statement categories produced winners most often? Named examples.

### Takeaway
Across 2023-2026 events where winners are public, winners cluster in (a) vertical, workflow-embedded agents (finance risk, fraud, healthcare, maintenance, permitting, sales ops) and (b) agent-payment/commerce infrastructure at crypto-sponsored events. Merchant/SMB tools and consumer PFM appear in India winner lists but mostly as mid-ranked or older (2022) winners; fraud/inclusion problems dominate RBI's list. I found no verified Paytm/PhonePe/Razorpay/Juspay/NPCI AI-era winner list, so India corporate-fintech evidence is thin.

### Inferences
- Winning categories by frequency in the lists above: (1) vertical operations agents (supply chain, permits, maintenance, incident management, sales dev), (2) regulator/problem-owner-defined fintech problems (fraud, mule accounts, inclusion) at RBI, (3) agent-payments infrastructure at crypto events, (4) public-interest/health/education. Generic consumer PFM tools won only in 2022 and were not top-ranked in 2025-26 lists.
- For a Paytm merchant track, the closest winning analogue is a vertical workflow agent that closes a loop on merchant money (settlements, collections, credit) with guardrails. This is inference, not a published Paytm judging statement.
- Winners were disproportionately domain insiders (cardiologist, lawyer, road engineer, physician, maintenance engineers) in Anthropic events, and companies with an existing product at RBI. Domain credibility is a repeated pattern.

### Gaps

## Q2. What do judges at corporate-sponsored hackathons say they score?

### Takeaway
Published rubrics converge on impact/usability, technical implementation/execution, innovation/originality, and (for agent events) functionality and guardrails. Corporate hosts add sponsor alignment (ROI for the sponsor). Judges and winners repeatedly say working demo beats slides and that narrative clarity matters as much as code.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft AI Agents Hackathon: innovation, impact, usability, solution quality, category alignment — [Microsoft](https://microsoft.github.io/AI_Agents_Hackathon/winners/)
- OpenAI-style Build Week rubric (via a blog): technical implementation, design and UX, potential impact, quality of the idea — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/jsb-securedme/what-ai-hackathon-winners-actually-have-in-common-1kla) (author's checklist: explain in one minute? demo shows behaviour? AI necessary? user clear? limits honest?)
- Salesforce TDX winners credit "alignment with Salesforce's goals", a clear use case and narrative; they asked "how would this provide ROI for Salesforce and help tell the Agentforce story"; they note projects with altruistic intent but little benefit to Salesforce "may have lost ground"; "It's really about how well you convey the concept." — [Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/how-team-agent-halo-won-100k-at-the-tdx-agentforce-hackathon/) (winner opinion, not official criteria)
- Search summary of AI-agent rubrics: one rubric weights functionality 40%, another security/guardrails 25% for finance and healthcare, generic rubrics put presentation 15% and UI/UX 10%; an NYC rubric scores low a project that mentions agents without a functional demo — [AI Tinkerers rubric](https://nyc.aitinkerers.org/hackathons/h_1de1QREJyuU/entries/@sowcantcode), [TinyFish rubric](https://sg-hackathon.tinyfish.ai/judging), [JCurve agentic AI hackathon](https://jcurve.gitbook.io/jcurve/agentic-ai-hackathon/judging-criteria-and-rewards) (cited from search-engine summary; pages not opened, so weights are medium confidence).
- Devfolio (Indian hackathon platform) describes its AI judging as human-written rubrics scoring each criterion 0-10 with weights; sample criteria Originality, Technical Execution, AI Depth; claims versus code are checked (example: "multi-tenant isolation" claimed but not implemented; "custom architecture" that is a pretrained model from a library) — [Devfolio](https://devfolio.co/blog/the-discerning-machine/). Illustrative examples, not real scores.
- Google Gen AI Exchange organisers' stated focus: "practical, real-world impact", production-readiness that "plug into existing enterprise pipelines", auditability, scalability for public sector, "workflow augmentation rather than novelty" — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/artificial-intelligence/google-cloud-gen-ai-hackathon-2025-winners-use-cases-and-what-270000-developers-built)
- Judge interviews: Rahul Tarak (decade of judging, Cal Hacks 12.0): "Either it runs in front of the judges or it doesn't"; "You can't hide behind a slide deck" — [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/hub4business/does-it-actually-work-rahul-taraks-decade-at-the-judging-table). Semgrep judge: "The best projects weren't the ones that built the most. They were the ones that built the right thing"; strongest teams state the problem in one sentence — [Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/the-ai-hackathon-effect). DEV judge: execution clarity, before/after story, honest AI use, security/error handling/deployment readiness, README/architecture/demo video — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/amising6/what-i-learned-after-reviewing-many-ai-and-developer-projects-as-a-hackathon-judge-2g06).
- Anthropic MaestrIA winner: "The single most important thing I did was build an auditable 9-dimension eval"; ARIA team: "you can't prompt your way to knowing what a maintenance technician actually looks at." — [Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon) (winner quotes, not judge statements).
- Paytm's own Stage 1 template asks for problem, solution, tech stack, USP, impact and benefits, business model; finalists must submit prototype + demo video + presentation. — [Luma](https://luma.com/6afp5q46)

### Inferences
- Treat a Paytm rubric as likely: impact/business model for Paytm (merchant growth, revenue, risk), working prototype (not slides), technical depth/guardrails, originality. This is inferred from the Paytm template plus corporate-hackathon norms; no Paytm rubric is public.
- Sponsor-alignment effect (Salesforce) probably applies: show how the idea increases Paytm GMV/lending/Soundbox/PG usage.

### Gaps
- No interviews with Paytm, PhonePe, Razorpay, Juspay or NPCI hackathon judges found.
- Weights in the AI Tinkerers/TinyFish/JCurve rubrics were taken from search summaries only.

## Q3. Which categories are saturated, and what separates winners within them?

### Takeaway
Generic chat/RAG assistants and "AI agent" demos that are really chat windows are the crowded, losing pattern. Within crowded categories, winners show real actions with guardrails, domain data, measured evals, and a narrow, quantified use case.

### Cited Findings
- "A lot of the agent demos were a chat window with a nice coat of paint"; at Cal Hacks 12.0 "agents were everywhere" (article narration). Strong teams showed what the agent touched, what changed, and how to verify or undo it. — [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/hub4business/does-it-actually-work-rahul-taraks-decade-at-the-judging-table)
- Judge volunteer view: AI-generated ideas feel generic and judges favour original concepts; a team's own voice/camera presentation beats an AI voiceover — [Boorghani/MEXC repost of "From the Judge's Seat"](https://rss.boorghani.com/from-the-judges-seat-standing-out-in-hackathons-in-the-age-of-ai) (page itself 404/403; from search summary).
- Semgrep judge: with AI tooling, "every team had working code, polished UI, and real data", so a working demo is no longer a differentiator; point of view on the problem is — [Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/the-ai-hackathon-effect)
- Google Gen AI Exchange: even where 4,400 prototypes were built, the finals favoured "agentic systems and explainable models, rather than prototype-style demos" per Outlook Business's description — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/artificial-intelligence/google-cloud-gen-ai-hackathon-2025-winners-use-cases-and-what-270000-developers-built). Note the finalist list still contains RAG-style legal and career tools, so RAG apps can place when the vertical is concrete.
- Differentiators seen in winners: domain-grounded eval (MaestrIA 74% to 81%), verifier sub-agents (Tekton), prediction accuracy vs ground truth (Sim Francisco 70% vs 70.38%), pilots arranged (Medkit), a live deployment (Quay in Singapore), human-approval gates (cement agents, Saathi compliance layer, SingIt physical confirmation). Sources: [Anthropic 4.7 winners](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon), [First AI Movers](https://radar.firstaimovers.com/ai-hackathon-winners-lessons-european-sme-2026), [Hack2Skill](https://blog.hack2skill.com/gen-ai-exchange-hackathon-finale), [Sui](https://www.sui.io/blog/sui-overflow-2026-winners.md), [Algorand](https://algorand.co/blog/agentic-commerce-x402-hackathon-berlin-recap).
- Weak patterns named by a judge: beautiful UI with limited technical depth, strong engineering with thin documentation, ambitious platforms lacking proof of usability, interesting ideas with unclear execution — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/amising6/what-i-learned-after-reviewing-many-ai-and-developer-projects-as-a-hackathon-judge-2g06)

### Inferences
- In India fintech, the saturated pile is likely "ask your transactions" PFM chatbots, loan-eligibility chatbots, and merchant dashboards with an LLM summary. Indirect evidence: Google Pay shipped "Ask Google Pay" (Gemini chatbot for UPI spend insights, July 2026) as a product, which commoditises that idea — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/google-pay-introduces-ai-powered-ask-google-pay-track-spending-understand-sips-and-access-more-credit-546164-2026-07-30). No source directly ranks saturation by category.
- Separators likely to carry weight at Paytm: use of real Paytm primitives (Soundbox, PG sandbox, settlements, UPI), vernacular voice (Sarvam), compliance gates (RBI/TRAI-style calling windows, consent), and quantified rupee impact with an honest baseline. Inference from Saathi/DukaanSaathi repos plus judge comments.

### Gaps
- No data on the share of submissions per category at any Indian fintech hackathon; no statement from any Paytm judge about saturation.
- Saathi repo flags that judges will question its "60%" figure and has no real-world outcome — an example of an unsupported-number risk, not an evidence of a loss.

## Q4. Evidence that agentic ("takes actions") AI wins versus advisory copilots, 2025-2026

### Takeaway
Evidence is consistent but mostly circumstantial: top prizes at Google ADK, Microsoft, Anthropic, Salesforce and agent-payment events went to systems that execute multi-step workflows (create tickets, file permits, raise work orders, pay), and sponsor-defined tracks (Paytm Track 3, Mastercard challenge) explicitly demand autonomous action. No study measures win rate agentic vs advisory, and advisory/copilot entries also won (WorkWizee is a "copilot" that executes actions).

### Cited Findings
- Paytm Track 3 wording requires "decisions, take actions, deliver measurable outcomes" — [Luma](https://luma.com/6afp5q46)
- Google ADK Grand Prize SalesShortcut: multi-agent system that does lead research, proposals and outreach — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/adk-hackathon-results-winners-and-highlights)
- Microsoft: WorkWizee (copilot that performs Jira/Bitbucket/Confluence actions) won Copilots; Best Overall RiskWise is an analysis system with natural-language querying (advisory). — [Microsoft](https://microsoft.github.io/AI_Agents_Hackathon/winners/)
- Anthropic: CrossBeam (parallel sub-agents produce permit action plans and draft correction letters), ARIA (agents generate work orders), Medkit (agentic grader), Best Use of Claude Managed Agents category exists — [Anthropic 4.6](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon), [4.7](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon)
- Mastercard-judged challenge required an agent that pays a verified merchant autonomously under a spending cap; winner "Wraps" — [Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/news-and-trends/stories/2026/36-hours-to-build-trust-in-machine-driven-commerce.html) (403; via search summary)
- Judge criterion: "Did it actually do something, or did it just talk about doing something?" — [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/hub4business/does-it-actually-work-rahul-taraks-decade-at-the-judging-table)
