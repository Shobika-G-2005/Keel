# Back Track 2 With a Lending Agent

**Recommendation: pick Track 2 (AI-Powered Financial Journeys) and build a lending-first, Tamil-first agent that guides a Paytm merchant from loan offer to repayment, with the compliance rules enforced in code.** Across AI and fintech hackathons whose winners are public, the winning problem type is a *vertical workflow agent that acts on a real system of record, runs inside hard guardrails, and proves its result with measured evals*. Generic chatbots, dashboards and "ask your transactions" assistants are the crowded losing pile. Every track can be built that way, so the track choice mostly comes down to crowding and sponsor fit. Public repos from this series show Track 3 as the most crowded with strong teams (6 of about 10 repos found), Track 1 as full of near-identical Hindi voice-and-udhaar builds, and Track 2 as nearly empty (1 repo), even though a self-reported Mumbai winner looks like a Track 2 journey. Track 2 is also tied to Paytm's fastest-growing line: financial-services distribution revenue grew 52% in FY26 and merchant loan distribution is one of four stated FY27 growth engines. The evidence behind this is thin. No winners are published for this series, most sources are self-reported, and the Chennai edition's own page could not be read, so treat the recommendation as a reasoned bet, not a finding.

## Winners are vertical agents that act, with guardrails and proof

Where winners are public, the pattern is consistent. The Google ADK grand prize went to SalesShortcut, a multi-agent sales development rep that researches leads, writes proposals and does outreach ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/adk-hackathon-results-winners-and-highlights)). Microsoft's Copilots winner WorkWizee turns P1/P2 incidents into Jira, Bitbucket and Confluence actions ([Microsoft](https://microsoft.github.io/AI_Agents_Hackathon/winners/)). Anthropic's events rewarded CrossBeam (permit-correction workflow), Medkit and ARIA (agents raising factory work orders), and one winner credited "an auditable 9-dimension eval" as the single most important thing he built ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon)). In India, the RBI's HaRBInger 2024 prizes went to regulator-defined fraud, mule-account and inclusion problems, all won by registered companies ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/rbi-announces-results-of-its-hackathon-2024-check-award-winners)). The Google Gen AI Exchange finals favoured agentic, explainable systems that "plug into existing enterprise pipelines" with auditability ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/artificial-intelligence/google-cloud-gen-ai-hackathon-2025-winners-use-cases-and-what-270000-developers-built)).

Winners in money-moving contexts also limit their own autonomy. Sup Wallet sets spending limits once and never holds the key, SingIt requires physical confirmation before money moves, and the Saathi finalist repo gates risky actions behind human approval ([Sui](https://www.sui.io/blog/sui-overflow-2026-winners.md), [Algorand](https://algorand.co/blog/agentic-commerce-x402-hackathon-berlin-recap), [Saathi repo](https://github.com/amithrh/saathi-paytm-hackathon)). Judges say what separates teams once everyone has a working demo. One judge said "every team had working code, polished UI, and real data," so the differentiator is a point of view on the problem ([Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/blog/2026/the-ai-hackathon-effect)). Another asks "Did it actually do something, or did it just talk about doing something?" ([Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/hub4business/does-it-actually-work-rahul-taraks-decade-at-the-judging-table)). Sponsor fit also scores: Salesforce's winners were asked how their idea would show ROI for the host ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/how-team-agent-halo-won-100k-at-the-tdx-agentforce-hackathon/)). This is a pattern across many events, not a measured win rate. Nobody has compared agentic and advisory entries, and the closest Paytm-specific signal is the Track 3 wording itself, "make decisions, take actions, and deliver measurable outcomes" ([Luma](https://luma.com/6afp5q46)). One point works against the pattern: Paytm's Pi enterprise agents and internal agents for collections, sales and support already exist ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/focused-view/paytm-intelligence-vijay-shekhar-sharmas-plan-to-turn-payments-scale-into-an-ai-business)), so judges will see through a generic Track 3 "teammate."

## Track 2 has the thinnest competition and a usable sponsor signal

The three tracks trade crowding against sponsor alignment. Track 1 aligns best with Paytm's strategy because the merchant base (1.64 crore subscription merchants) is the core asset, but Paytm already ships AI Soundbox and a Pi Sales & Marketing Agent. Public repos in this series (Munshi, VoiceVyapar, HarvestWise, DukaanSaathi) are also near-duplicates of each other ([Munshi](https://github.com/23f3003609/munshi-ai-Paytm-hackathon), [VoiceVyapar](https://github.com/smarpitm/Paytm_Ai_Hackathon)). Track 3 matches Paytm's cost-and-productivity story and its hiring push for agentic engineers. It is also where strong agent builders cluster: Saathi, Paytm Saathi (seven agents), Wapsi, MerchantPulse, Commerce OS and Pakka are all Track 3, and at least five follow the same "revive dormant merchants, chase udhaar, Hindi voice via Sarvam" shape ([Paytm Saathi](https://github.com/Karnita-Saumya/Paytm-Saathi), [Wapsi](https://github.com/amanunreal/Paytm-hack), [Pakka](https://github.com/sneharc16/Paytm-hack)). The notes' own judgment is that Track 3 has the most strong competitors per unit of opportunity and Track 2 the fewest.

Track 2's weakness is sponsor signal, and the notes show where it sits. Insurance is currently immaterial for Paytm: it has no insurance revenue in the mix and exited an insurer contract with a "materially large negative contribution margin" ([compoundingai.in](https://compoundingai.in/market-news/paytm-q2-fy27-earnings-call), an aggregator). Lending is the opposite. Financial-services distribution revenue was **Rs 2,593 crore in FY26 (+52%)** ([Paytm IR](https://paytm.com/blog/investor-relations/key-takeaways-from-our-q4fy26-earnings-call/)) and **Rs 912 crore in Q2 FY27 (+49%)** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/paytm-q2-fy27-results-net-profit-jumps-to-225-crore-as-revenue-rises-285)). The largest quantified pain point is credit risk: financial-guarantee (DLG) expense of **Rs 180 crore in H1 FY27**, on Rs 940 crore of DLG limits ([compoundingai.in](https://compoundingai.in/market-news/paytm-q2-fy27-results-analysis), an aggregator, unverified). That is why the recommendation is **lending-first, not insurance-first**. The track keeps the low crowding while the idea ties to a P&L line Paytm management talks about. The self-reported Mumbai winner, "Paytm Saathi – Money SOS" (a single-button urgent-money flow where insurance might cover part of a hospital deposit), is the only winner signal. It is a consumer insurance-plus-credit journey, not a merchant bot ([LinkedIn result](https://it.linkedin.com/in/angelozabelli)). Treat that as weak confirmation. The name "Saathi" is shared by several unrelated entries, so do not assume the winner resembles the Track 3 merchant repos.

## The winning angle: a compliance-by-construction merchant loan journey

Build one end-to-end journey for a Chennai small merchant, working name "Thunai". The three stages share a single policy engine and audit log.

Stage one is the offer. The agent reads the merchant's seeded settlement history, computes a working-capital pre-offer in code (never in the LLM), and explains it in spoken Tamil with the Key Fact Statement and cooling-off window, like BharatPe's vernacular Credit Coach ([Analytics India Mag](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-news/bharatpe-brings-agentic-ai-to-merchants-with-new-ai-suite)).

Stage two is onboarding. The agent collects consent, completes the mandate and links the disbursal.

Stage three is the differentiator, early-stress servicing. Paytm's merchant loans average about Rs 2 lakh with repayment near Rs 500 a day ([compoundingai.in](https://compoundingai.in/market-news/paytm-q2-fy27-earnings-call), an aggregator), so a settlement dip, an offline Soundbox or a festival lull shows up in the data before a missed payment does. The agent detects the dip, offers a pre-approved hold or reschedule inside a lender-defined envelope, completes any payment in-call through a UPI link, and escalates distress or disputes to a human with a summary.

This is deliberately different from the crowded pattern. Most public entries chase dormant merchants to *sell* something; this one protects a loan book, which is the DLG line Paytm cares about. Mahindra Finance and Sarvam already run 1 crore+ voice collection calls and Gnani and Razorpay already close UPI payments in-call, so the build must not claim novelty on "voice collections." The claim is that the *compliance and measurement layer* is visible and the journey spans offer to servicing ([Analytics India Mag](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-news/mahindra-finance-sarvam-scale-voice-ai-to-over-1-cr-calls), [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/gnaniai-partners-with-razorpay-to-enable-real-time-payment-collections-via-ai-voice-agents-518095-2026-02-26)). Pick a Chennai trade the existing repos skip (HarvestWise already did Tamil vegetable vendors), such as tea stalls, textile retail or auto-parts. Do not let the agent make a final adverse credit decision. The RBI's FREE-AI report stresses explainability and oversight, and offer eligibility should come from a stated lender rule the agent applies and explains ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2025/08/rbi-free-ai-committee-report-on-framework-for-responsible-and-ethical-enablement-of-artificial-intelligence.html)).

### The demo that proves it

The notes' demo arc fits this build, and the whole thing runs in about three minutes. Open with one number, such as a seeded merchant whose settlements fell 40% in a week. Run a live spoken Tamil call that ends in a visible state change in the mock systems: restructure recorded, UPI link paid, ticket closed. Trigger one deliberate escalation to show the handoff summary and the audit trail. Then show a dashboard from an eval run of 50 to 100 scripted scenarios, including adversarial ones (prompt injection, hallucinated clause, angry borrower, out-of-hours call). It reports autonomous resolution rate with its definition, escalation rate by reason code, policy violations blocked (target zero), and simulated roll-rate against a rules-only baseline. Label every figure synthetic. The strongest repos in the series already do this: a number audit that checks every rupee figure the agent speaks against tool output (Saathi), a 14-attack red team with zero successes (Pakka), and an explicit assumption-based business case (MerchantPulse). Klarna's 2025 reversal, when its CEO said cost had been too dominant and quality suffered ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396)), is the reason to show failure cases and clean human handoff rather than only a resolution rate.

For the stack, use a tool-calling model (Claude or Gemini) behind an MCP server wrapping mock services: merchant ledger and settlements, UPI payment link, loan store, ticketing, and a WhatsApp or SMS sender. Use Sarvam Saaras and Bulbul for Tamil speech through LiveKit or Pipecat, with a text fallback and a pre-recorded clip. Put a deterministic policy layer outside the LLM covering amount caps, calling hours, consent, PII redaction and the no-final-adverse-decision rule, plus an append-only audit log. Use the Paytm Payment Gateway sandbox or Paytm MCP server if accessible on site; Saathi used the sandbox, but the official MCP page could not be read, so mock it and say so ([Paytm docs](https://paytmpayments.com/docs/mcp-server-overview)).

## Pitch deck: seven sections, sponsor-numbers first

The organizer fixes the seven sections: Title, Problem, Solution, Tech stack, USP, Impact & benefits, Business model ([Luma](https://luma.com/6afp5q46)). Judging criteria are unpublished, so the content below is inference from the template plus norms at corporate hackathons.

| Section | What to put in it |
|---|---|
| Title | Product name, track, a one-line promise ("keeps a merchant's loan healthy, in Tamil, inside the rules"), team |
| Problem | One quantified Paytm pain: DLG expense of Rs 180 crore in H1 FY27 and merchant loan distribution as an FY27 growth engine; the gap that only 3.6 crore of 8.7 crore MSMEs have ever accessed formal credit ([CIBIL-SIDBI](https://www.transunioncibil.com/content/dam/transunion-cibil/corporate/documents/msme-pulse-report-july-2026.pdf)); plus one named Chennai merchant story |
| Solution | An agent-loop diagram (trigger, tool call, policy check, action, verification, escalation), not a feature list; show the three stages |
| Tech stack | Name the deterministic policy layer, audit log and eval harness first, the LLM second; say what is real versus simulated in the room |
| USP | One contrast sentence against the obvious alternative, in the spirit of "Other voice bots wait for questions" |
| Impact & benefits | A worked rupee example per merchant, with every assumption visible, and the KPI set (autonomous resolution, escalations by reason, violations blocked, simulated roll-rate change); no unsupported percentages |
| Business model | Value to Paytm, not a startup market-size slide: lower DLG and ECL cost, higher loan conversion, retained subscriptions; rollout inside Soundbox, the Paytm for Business app and WhatsApp, matching Paytm's line that a merchant "does not necessarily need another standalone technology interface" ([Paytm blog](https://paytm.com/blog/artificial-intelligence/why-paytm-is-building-applied-ai-not-just-another-chatbot/)); outcome-based pricing precedent such as Intercom Fin's about $0.99 per resolution ([Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/ai-pulse/ai-pulse-2026-08-02-intercom-s-fin-ai-agent-is-nearing-100m-arr-roughly-half-of)) |

Keep it to seven slides, since series teams used seven to nine, and add a one-page worked example. The Saathi README itself flagged a missing rupee example and an unsupported "60%" figure as open items, so lead with a worked example and cite only numbers you can source ([Saathi](https://github.com/amithrh/saathi-paytm-hackathon)).

## What the evidence cannot tell you

Start with what is missing. **No official winners, scores, rubric or per-track submission counts exist for any 2026 Paytm edition.** The notes found about a dozen public GitHub READMEs, all with zero stars and none reporting a placement, plus one LinkedIn winner claim (about 6,000 registrations, 100 teams offline, 10 on stage) that could not be opened and rests on search-result extracts. Those repos are a biased sample: only teams that published a README appear, so the real shortlist pool is unknown, and Track 2's apparent emptiness could mean strong teams judged it unattractive rather than overlooked. Several sponsor figures (DLG expense, loan ticket size, Q2 call guidance) come from an aggregator the notes rate low-reliability, with inconsistent call dates. Company numbers are unaudited, and vendor resolution rates differ in definition.

The edition itself is uncertain. The only fetched Luma page is the Bengaluru listing, marked "Past Event," with a deck deadline of 27 October and finals on 31 October. A Chennai page exists on devSemble but its date, deadline, prizes and shortlist status could not be retrieved, and the notes list Hyderabad on 13-14 November. If Chennai followed Bengaluru's schedule, its Stage 1 deck deadline has passed. **Verify today whether your team is shortlisted.** If it is not, ask the organizers about a late slot or the finalist waitlist; if it is, the deck becomes your opening presentation. Also check what APIs or sandbox Paytm supplies, which the notes could not confirm. Finally, RBI's recovery directions disagree across sources on effective date (1 July 2026, October 2026 or 1 January 2027) and on device-locking scope, so cite the rules as "reported" until checked against RBI text ([Free Press Journal](https://freepressjournal.in/business/rbi-new-loan-recovery-rules-from-2027-banks-cannot-harass-borrowers-emi-calls-restricted-to-8-am7-pm)). The DPDP consent-manager provisions take effect on 13 November 2026 and the main obligations around May 2027 ([AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/)), so keep consent logging in the design.

## Conclusion

The useful insight is that the track and the winning pattern are separable. The pattern (act on a system of record, enforce policy in code, prove it with evals) is what Track 3 asks for and what the best public entries already do, but nothing stops a team from applying it inside Track 2, where the crowd is thinner. The risk of that bet is sponsor fit, which the lending-first framing is chosen to buy back. If the team sees the room on the day and finds Track 2 stuffed with copies of the Mumbai "Money SOS" idea, the same engine and eval harness port to Track 3 as a loan-servicing teammate with little rework, so the downside is bounded. That is a contingency, not a second recommendation: commit to Track 2.
